Each cylinder is drawn for you except one thing: the far end is still a flat line. Drag the dot out along the barrel until that end is as round as it really is, then lock it in. Four cylinders, then a score.

The rule: the far end of a cylinder is always rounder than the near end — never flatter, never the same. The whole drill is judging how much rounder.

Scored on roundness alone — how tall the end is compared to how wide. Exactly right is 100 and the score fades to 0 as you drift; the two faint dotted ends in the reveal are where it runs out.

The reveal draws the true end over yours after every cylinder, and at the end of the round it says which way you leaned — that habit is the thing worth fixing.

Studio tip: the flatter the near end looks, the more the barrel is pointing away from you, and the bigger the jump to the far end.
