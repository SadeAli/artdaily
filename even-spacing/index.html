$ man even-spacing

Two lines, then two boxes. Cross each line with short ticks that split it into equal parts; then fill each box with evenly spaced parallel strokes — the dashed line inside the box shows the direction to run them, which on the tilted box is also the answer to “which edge is the short one”. Artists call this hatching.

Nothing scores itself. Place your marks, look at them, undo ↩ anything you want to move, and press done ✓ when you are happy. Every mark is its own stroke, so lifting between them costs nothing — a trackpad is perfectly at home here.

Hold the reveal to study it. After each item the pink marks show what even would have looked like: on a line, a bar runs from each perfect tick to where yours actually landed, labelled in pixels; in a box, a pink ghost fill sits under your own strokes with your gap sizes written between them. Press and hold anywhere to keep it up for as long as you like, and release to move on.

Scoring is pure geometry: how close your ticks sit to the even split, how parallel your strokes stay, and how much the gaps between them wander. Tolerances are measured in pixels and never get stricter than a real hand can hit — and they widen for a mouse or a finger (the corner of the HUD says which the drill graded for). On a phone the box is bigger and asks for fewer strokes, so the gaps stay wider than the fingertip placing them.

Studio tip: even spacing comes from the arm, not the eye. Move the same distance each time and let the marks land where they land — counting a steady 1-2, 1-2 as you go works better than measuring each gap.

Splitting by eye, then holding a pitch

Four items make a round. Two baselines to cross with evenly spaced ticks, three then five; then two boxes to fill with even parallel strokes, the second tilted about 30° with a dashed guide showing which way its strokes should run. Nothing scores itself: the item waits for done ✓, and undo takes back the last tick, so a poor third tick is not locked in. After each one the ideal appears in pink beside your ink, offsets and gaps labelled in pixels, with a plain-words read: “your gaps widened as you went”.

What is graded here is mostly placement, not motor smoothness — only the parallelism of the fill strokes is a motor term, and a mouse places about as well as a pen. It is the judgement behind even hatching, a row of ticks on an axis, or any repeat that must hold its pitch. Because the reveal labels misses in pixels and names the drift in words, what you carry to the next item is the tendency, not the score.

Two numbers behind every tolerance

A tolerance here is whichever of two numbers turns out bigger: the accuracy the drill wants, taken against the gap it is judging, or the accuracy a hand on your hardware can actually deliver in pixels. On the ticks, 4% of a gap or 3px is free and the score runs out at 40% of a gap or an eased 16px. On the fills, parallelism runs out at an eased 14° RMS — the one term eased outright — and rhythm, the spread of your gaps, at 42% of the mean gap or an eased 5px. That pixel floor matters: the five-tick item once hit zero at 14px on a phone, narrower than the fingertip placing the ticks, and now zeroes at 24px for a finger, where an attempt 10px out reads 72 rather than 30. Sheets under 460px also get a wider box and ask for 5–7 strokes, not 8–12.
