Two mannequins in the same pose. On one of them a single body part is the wrong size — too long, too short, too big or too wide compared with the rest of that same figure. Tap that figure, then tap the part that is off. Five items; the errors get subtler (±30% → ±25% → ±20%). Item 1 is always one of the four big parts — head, shoulders, torso or a leg — so the opener is a flaw you can see across the sheet.

Scoring: naming the right figure = 35, and up to 65 more the closer your second tap lands on the bad part — finding the part is the skill, so it carries most of the marks. Get the figure wrong and nothing is lost: the drill says “not that one”, you take the other for 10 instead of 35, and the part is still worth its full 65. The head-height ruler is on screen the whole time, not just at the end, because the whole method is measuring rather than eyeballing. The score in the HUD is your running mean across the items played so far.

The reveal draws a dashed skeleton over the flawed figure: that is the same pose built to the canon, so wherever the dashes and the drawing come apart is exactly the error — with the bad part highlighted and tagged with how far off it was.

No drawing here on purpose — this one trains the error-spotting eye you proof your own figures with.

Studio tip: measure against the landmarks printed under the picture rather than trusting the overall impression — navel at 3 heads, elbows at the waist, wrists at the crotch line.
