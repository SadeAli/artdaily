Freehand a 3D box, one straight stroke per edge — 6 edges minimum, a full box is 9–12 — then hit “check it”. Lift between edges; that is how this one is meant to be drawn. “Undo” takes back the last stroke; “clear” starts the box over without spending the round.

What a vanishing point is. Every edge you draw is extended past its ends the moment you finish it — watch where those extensions go. Edges that run the same way in 3D all point at one shared spot on the page: that spot is their vanishing point. A box has edges going three ways, so it has three of them.

Checking sorts your strokes into those three sets and asks of each: do these really aim at one point? Tight agreement scores high. Parallel is fine — it just means the point is a very long way off. Edges that spread apart as they go back are the classic mistake, because going away from you they should be closing in.

Tap any row of the critique to show just that set on the sheet. The dashed ghost is a true box worked out from your two best sets — the shape you were reaching for.

How much wobble is allowed depends on what you are drawing with and how big your box is — a short edge turns the same hand tremor into a much bigger angle. The HUD says which input it scored for, and your score is only ever compared with your own.

Studio tip: after every single line you draw, extend it and look at where it points. That habit is the whole exercise.

Drawing a box and checking where its edges point

You draw a box freehand, one straight stroke per edge — six at minimum, nine to twelve for a full one, lifting between them. Each edge is extended past its own ends the moment it is accepted, so where a line actually points is visible from stroke one. Checking sorts the strokes into three families by the vanishing point each set shares, marks those points and draws the box the implied camera would have seen. Tapping a critique row isolates one set.

The habit underneath is small: extend a line, look at where it goes. It answers the question a page of practice boxes cannot answer about itself — are my boxes converging? Edges that spread apart as they recede are the classic error — going away from you they should be closing in — which the drawn segment hides and the extension shows. Sorting goes by shared vanishing point rather than by angle on the page: under ordinary perspective one 3D direction can spread over 40° or more of image angle, which is enough to defeat any sort that goes by angle on the page.

How honestly a set converges

Each family is scored on how tightly its lines agree on one point; the round is the mean of the three, scaled by how complete the box is. Tolerance is stated in pixels of slop and converted to degrees at the edge lengths you drew: 6px of wobble is 2.3° on a 150px edge but 4.9° on a 70px one. It widens for the input in hand and never tightens past the old pen-tablet standard. The three sets are then held to what a box cannot fake: three mutually perpendicular directions seen through a pinhole leave three vanishing points on a triangle, and the camera's principal point has to be that triangle's orthocenter, with one focal length dropping out of it that fits all three pairs. If no real camera fits, those sets are not one box, however neatly any one converges. No AI and no model reads the drawing — the scoring is closed-form geometry: each stroke is reduced to its best-fit line, the lines are sorted by the point they share, and every number above is measured off those lines.
