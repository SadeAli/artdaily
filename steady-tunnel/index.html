Press in the coral ring and steer down the tunnel to the flag. Near enough to the ring counts — a press just outside is pulled onto it rather than refused. Your ink flashes coral the instant it crosses a wall.

You may lift. Reaching the flag in one drag is more than a trackpad can physically do, so a lift is not an ending: press again near where your ink stopped and the same run carries on. A run that never reaches the flag is never scored — it just waits for you, and the hint says how far you got.

Two things make the score, and the reveal shows you both: inside is the share of your line that stayed between the walls, centred is how close to the middle it ran. Staying inside is worth three times as much as hugging the middle, so a run that never touched a wall reads as the clean run it was.

Hold the reveal to study it. When your run is scored, the dashes appear down the middle of the tunnel and every coral stretch is a piece of your line that the score counted as outside — press and hold anywhere to keep that picture up for as long as you like, and release to move on.

Three tunnels per round, each narrower — the round is their average. The walls are set for whatever you are drawing with, and the HUD says which: a mouse is judged as a mouse, not as a pen, and a phone gets a corridor sized for a fingertip instead of a desktop's.

Studio tip: keep your eyes ahead of your line — steer toward where the tunnel is going, not where you already are.

Steering a line instead of hoping

A corridor winds across the sheet and narrows as it goes. You press inside the coral ring at its mouth and steer your line to the flag at the far end; any part of the line that leaves the corridor turns coral under your hand as it happens. Lifting is allowed, since reaching the flag in one drag is further than a trackpad physically travels — press again near where the ink stopped and the same run carries on, and a run that never reaches the flag is not scored at all. Three tunnels make a round, each tighter and curvier than the last.

The corridor is a smooth spline with its walls offset at a steadily shrinking half-width, which makes the middle of the tunnel exact ground truth, not an impression. What it asks of you is steering: looking at where the path is going instead of where your line already is, and committing to a curve while the room for error keeps closing. The slot is a fraction of canvas height and is widened for whatever you are drawing with — a fingertip needs more room than a pen — but the wiggle is widened alongside it, so an easier corridor still has to be steered rather than crossed with a straight line.

What inside and centred are worth

Two terms make the number, and the reveal prints both: inside, the share of your line that stayed between the walls, and centred, how close to the middle it ran — inside 92% · centred 71% · 84. Inside counts for three times as much as centring, 0.75 against 0.25, so a run that never touched a wall lands at 87.5 and reads as the clean run it was. Coverage is a gate you are told about, not a silent multiplier, and the round is the average of the three tunnels.
