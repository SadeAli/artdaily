Slide each paint's share until your mix matches the target, then lock it in. Every colour opens on a deliberately-off blend, so there is always a gap to close. Only the balance between sliders matters — 80/40 mixes the same colour as 40/20 — and the % beside each slider shows its real share. The ± buttons step by exactly 1 — hold one down and it runs, the same way holding an arrow key on a focused slider does. Four colours per round; the last two add a third paint.

Scored by ΔE — how far apart your eye reads the two halves. Under 3 they look identical; 26 apart scores zero. The reveal shows the true ratios next to yours — of each pair of bars the flat one on top is your share and the taller outlined one below it is the true share — and says which way the mix missed (too light, too warm…).

Studio tip: get the lightness right first. “squint ◐” drops the colour out of both halves and leaves only light and dark — it is available the whole time, not just after you lock in.

What mixing to a target teaches

The bench holds two swatches side by side — the target colour, and whatever your current blend reads as — with one ratio slider beneath it for each base pigment, named for what it looks like rather than given a bare letter. A round is four colours: two bases to begin with, a third pigment for the last two, and a closing pair of hues so near each other that the only dependable way to separate them is by value. Every colour opens on a deliberately wrong blend, at least ΔE 12 from the target, so no colour ever opens on an accidental match.

What the drill measures is ratio judgement — not which pigments belong in a mix, but how much of each one it really takes. Sliders are relative shares, so 80/40 makes the same colour as 40/20, and the percentage beside each row reports the balance you have actually set. Mixing runs on a subtractive, pigment-style model, and every target is generated through that same model, so an exact match always exists on the sliders. After a lock-in the true ratios appear beside yours, and the Lab deltas are read back as plain direction — too light, a touch too warm.

Where the ΔE number comes from

Each colour is scored on ΔE (CIE76), the distance in Lab between your mix and the target, converted by 100 × (1 − ΔE/26). That is the whole judge — a formula, not a model, which is also why the true ratios can be printed beside yours. Below about 3 the two halves stop reading as different colours; 26 apart is worth nothing. A round's score is the mean of its four, carried live in the HUD as you work. Because the true ratios are rounded to whole percents, the correct answer always lands on a notch a hand can actually reach, which is what keeps 100 attainable; locking the opening blend without moving anything averages around 18.
