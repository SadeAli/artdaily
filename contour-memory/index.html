A blob flashes, then hides. Redraw its outline (its contour) from memory — any number of strokes, anywhere, lifting as often as you like — then press “done ✓”. “undo” drops the last stroke; “clear” wipes them all. Three figures per round, each shown shorter and shaped trickier.

Scoring compares your shape to the real one, wherever you drew it; drawing it far too small or big costs a little, and “peek −8” reshows the figure once at exactly that price.

The drill also eases its tolerance for what you are drawing with — pen, mouse or finger — and the HUD says which. Your score is only ever compared with your own.

Studio tip: don’t try to memorize every wiggle. Pick 3 landmarks — the widest bump, the deepest dent, the sharpest corner — and keep the shape between them true.

Each figure's reveal moves on by itself after a pause — but the pause is a floor, not a clock. A quick tap jumps ahead, and a press you keep down holds the reveal for as long as your finger does.

Drawing a shape after it has gone

A smooth blob appears on the sheet for a moment, then hides, and the outline — the contour — has to go back on the sheet from memory, in as many strokes as you want, wherever they land, lifting whenever you need to. Three figures per round, each harder than the last, the exposure shrinking from about 2.8 seconds to 1.1 and the shape growing from six control points to ten with deeper dents in it. One “peek −8” per figure brings it back for a moment at exactly that price; “undo” drops your last stroke, “clear” wipes them all.

Your strokes are moved and resized onto the true contour before anything is measured, so where you drew it is not being tested — only whether you held the shape. What is left to test is whether the outline survived the moment the blob went away. Banking every wiggle fails at 1.1 seconds; three landmarks — the widest bump, the deepest dent, the sharpest corner — with the shape kept true between them does not. Each figure ends with the real contour redrawn over your strokes, so the drift is visible.

Where the number comes from

After that alignment, a symmetric chamfer distance scores the shape — each outline measured against the other, with the worse of the two directions the one that counts — and drawing far off-scale costs a gentle extra. The chamfer at which the shape score hits zero is 0.11 of the true figure's diagonal, or a pixel floor if that is larger, plus a little slop — both pixel terms eased for what you are drawing with, so a phone sheet is no stricter than a desktop one and a fingertip is not held to a nib's precision. No model looks at your drawing: the blob was generated by the drill, so its true outline is known exactly, and every number above is arithmetic on the distance between that outline and your strokes. Scores are only ever compared with your own.
