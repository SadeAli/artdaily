The grey line through A is the reference — it is labelled on the canvas. Each prompt asks for a stroke turned so many degrees from it, and says which way to turn in words. The amber arrow at A turns the same way, so you can check yourself before you commit.

You do not have to start at A. Press anywhere on the sheet and pull. Only the direction of your stroke is scored, so the drill slides your line onto A for you — there is nothing here to miss and nothing that can refuse your press.

Roughly 2° off is a perfect 100 and points fall to zero at about 27° off. On a short pull a couple of stray pixels really are a couple of degrees, so the free zone widens as the pull gets shorter, and widens again for a mouse or trackpad — the corner of the HUD says which hardware the drill graded for. A round is the average of six strokes, and the asked angles get subtler as you go.

Turn the right amount the wrong way and the drill says so — "right size, wrong way round" — and hands the item back free, once per item. Misreading which way to turn is not the same mistake as misjudging an angle.

After each stroke the true ray and an amber arc appear: the arc is a protractor, exactly the one from school, measuring from the reference to the truth. Press and hold to keep it on screen as long as you like; let go for the next one.

Studio tip: don't measure — commit. Your first read of an angle is usually within 10°; second-guessing drags it wider.

Reading an angle off a reference line

Each item gives an anchor dot, a grey reference line through it, and a prompt in plain words: 45° counter-clockwise from that line, say. An amber arrow at the anchor turns the same way, so the direction is checkable before you commit. Then you pull one stroke, from anywhere on the sheet — only direction counts, so the drill slides your line onto the anchor. The true ray appears with a protractor arc and your error in degrees; hold the press to study it, release for the next of six. The opener always sets the reference flat or upright.

What this trains is reading and committing, not a steady hand. Direction is fitted across every sample of the pull, so two strokes that share a principal axis are graded identically, whether one shook out of a mouse and the other ran dead straight off a tablet: smoothness, speed and shake fall out of the maths. Turning the right amount the wrong way counts as its own mistake — land near the mirrored target and the drill calls it “right size, wrong way round” and gives the item back once, free.

From one pull to a number

The stroke's least-squares direction is compared with the target angle. Inside a free zone the marks are full; past it they fall in a straight line to zero at 27° of error. The 27° never widens for hardware: what it grades is the judgement, and no pointer makes that judgement better. The free zone is the hardware's: min(8°, 2° + atan(eased 2.5px / pull length)). It widens on short pulls — ±2.5px of jitter is ±3.6° over a 40px pull but ±1.2° over a 120px one — and widens again for a mouse or finger, which jitters about twice as far as a pen nib. A round is the mean of six strokes, and the angles asked for get finer as it runs.
