Thick where it lands, thin where it whispers

The top ribbon is one stroke that gets thicker and thinner along its length. Redraw it on the dashed guide below with the thicks and thins in the same places — five strokes per round.

How you ask for thickness. With a pen that reports pressure, press harder for a thicker line. With anything else — mouse, trackpad, finger — height does it: run along the dashes for a hairline, climb into the shaded band above them to lay weight on. Higher is thicker, and the dot under your pointer shows exactly how thick.

Lifting is free. A trackpad cannot cross the whole sheet in one press, so it does not have to. Lift, put the pointer back down anywhere, and carry on filling in the line — nothing is scored until the guide is inked end to end. “Start this stroke over” wipes the attempt with no penalty.

Scoring compares the shape of your thick-and-thin against the target's, not its absolute size, so drawing everything a bit thinner than the ribbon costs nothing. A stroke of one unvarying weight loses 20%. How much room the score allows depends on what you are drawing with — the HUD says which, and your score is only ever compared with your own.

Studio tip: weight is a decision. Pick where the stroke whispers and where it lands before you start pulling it.
