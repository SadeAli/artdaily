The top ribbon is one stroke that gets thicker and thinner along its length. Redraw it on the dashed guide below with the thicks and thins in the same places — five strokes per round.

How you ask for thickness. With a pen that reports pressure, press harder for a thicker line. With anything else — mouse, trackpad, finger — height does it: run along the dashes for a hairline, climb into the shaded band above them to lay weight on. Higher is thicker, and the dot under your pointer shows exactly how thick.

Lifting is free. A trackpad cannot cross the whole sheet in one press, so it does not have to. Lift, put the pointer back down anywhere, and carry on filling in the line — nothing is scored until the guide is inked end to end. “Start this stroke over” wipes the attempt with no penalty.

Scoring compares the shape of your thick-and-thin against the target's, not its absolute size, so drawing everything a bit thinner than the ribbon costs nothing. A stroke of one unvarying weight loses 20%. How much room the score allows depends on what you are drawing with — the HUD says which, and your score is only ever compared with your own.

Studio tip: weight is a decision. Pick where the stroke whispers and where it lands before you start pulling it.

Putting weight where you mean to

The ribbon along the top of the sheet is one stroke that swells and tapers as it goes: thick to thin, thin to thick, thin-thick-thin, two bumps, freehand. Below it sits a dashed guide, and the task is to lay that stroke down again with the thicks and the thins in the same places. Five strokes a round. How you ask for width depends on what is in your hand: a pen that reports pressure uses pressure, while a mouse, trackpad or finger uses height — ride the dashes for a hairline, climb into the shaded band above them to lay weight on.

The habit the drill goes after is the stroke of one unvarying weight, and it charges 20% for it. What replaces that is a decision taken before the pull: which stretch of the line lands and which whispers. Height carries width instead of speed because speed cannot be aimed — a drag is naturally slow-fast-slow, the inverse of nearly every target shape. Lifting is free as well, since your place is horizontal progress across the guide rather than distance travelled: press again anywhere and carry on until the guide is inked end to end.

How the two profiles are compared

Your width profile and the target's are sampled at 64 evenly spaced positions and min-max normalised, so what is graded is the shape of the thick-and-thin and not its absolute size — drawing the whole thing finer than the ribbon costs nothing. The two are then compared by RMS difference. The RMS at which the score reaches zero is eased per input mode and capped at 0.50, a measured cap rather than a taste: a stroke of one unvarying weight measures about 0.34 RMS and pure noise about 0.42, so putting the zero point beyond 0.5 would start handing marks to the very mistake the drill exists to correct. A pen keeps 0.34, mouse and trackpad and finger get 0.50, and the HUD says which it scored.
