Each picture traps one pocket of paper between its shapes — that pocket is the negative space. It breathes twice, then goes quiet: trace its outline right on top of the picture and press done ✓. “show again” replays the breath without touching your trace, “undo” drops your last stroke, “clear” wipes them all.

Lifting is free. Pick the line up near where you put it down and the drill reads the pieces as one line, so a short-throw trackpad or a small phone screen costs you nothing.

Scoring compares your line with the true edge of the space both ways, so close and complete beats fast. The tolerance has a pixel floor and is eased for pen, mouse or finger — the HUD says which — so a small screen is not a stricter drill. Three spaces per round, slimmer each time; the first one of a fresh round stays tinted while you trace.

Studio tip: name nothing. If you catch yourself thinking “mug”, half-close your eyes until the detail drops away and the picture is only dark shapes and light holes again.

Drawing the gap instead of the object

A round builds three small compositions — a mug with the hole in its handle, a pierced arch, interlocking arcs, leaning slabs — and each traps a single pocket of paper between its shapes. That pocket breathes twice in a wash of colour, then goes quiet, and you draw its outline straight onto the picture. Three spaces per round, each slimmer than the last; the first of a fresh round stays tinted while you trace it, and “show again” replays the breath without disturbing your line.

The habit this works against is naming. Once a shape has been filed as “mug”, the hand starts drawing the remembered mug instead of the one on screen. Trapped paper has no name to fall back on, so its edges have to be copied as they actually run. The gap polygon is generated first and the silhouettes are assembled around it, which makes the correct answer exact rather than inferred, and its narrow side is floored in real pixels, so no round hands you a gap narrower than the fingertip trying to trace it.

How your line is measured against the true edge

Your strokes and the densely sampled true boundary are compared with a symmetric chamfer distance, run both ways and then combined by whichever direction did worse, so a line has to be close to the edge and complete around it. Distance is measured from each point to the path itself rather than to the nearest sample, so a quick drag whose points landed far apart loses nothing to how often your device reports them. The chamfer that scores zero is the larger of 0.09 × the gap's diagonal or a fixed floor, plus slop, both eased by input mode so a phone is not a stricter drill. A lift shorter than roughly 6% of that diagonal joins two strokes into one line. No model reads your line: the gap polygon was generated before the shapes around it, so the true edge is known exactly, and the score is that distance comparison and nothing else.
