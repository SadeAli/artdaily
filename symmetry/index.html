$ man symmetry

The left half of a figure is drawn — draw its mirror image on the RIGHT of the dashed line, in as many strokes as you like, then press done ✓. Undo ↩ removes the last stroke, clear restarts the figure — no penalty. Lifting between strokes costs nothing at all, so short marks and a mouse or trackpad are perfectly at home here.

Starting is forgiving: press up to a hand's width left of the dashed line and the stroke slides onto it. Only a press well into the left half is turned away, because that half is the figure you are copying.

The thin bar under the figure is your ink. Redrawing the same curve over and over uses it up; when it runs out the drill stops taking ink and asks you to press done ✓ — it never scores you mid-stroke. The first 20 px of every stroke are free, so marking heights costs nothing.

Scored on how far your mirrored strokes land from the true curve — measured in pixels, in a band that grows with the figure but never gets stricter than a real hand can hit, and that widens for a mouse or a finger (the corner of the HUD says which the drill graded for). A careful mirror reaches 100 on any device. The reveal marks your widest misses with short whiskers; tap it to jump ahead. Keyboard: Enter = done / next, Backspace = undo.

Studio tip: don't copy the outline line by line. Pick three or four heights, look at how far the drawn half sits from the dashed line at each one, put a short mark that far out on the right — then join your marks. Symmetry is measured, not felt.

Each figure's reveal moves on by itself after a pause — but the pause is a floor, not a clock. A quick tap jumps ahead, and a press you keep down holds the reveal for as long as your finger does.

Symmetry you measure rather than feel

A dashed vertical line splits the sheet; one smooth half-figure is already drawn against it, and the missing half is yours, freehand, in as many strokes as you like. Lifting between strokes costs nothing, undo drops the last one, clear restarts the figure, and neither is penalised. The round runs three figures, each curvier than the one before. The thin bar under the sheet is ink: redrawing the same curve over and over spends it, the first 20px of every stroke are free so marking heights is cheap, and when it runs out the drill stops inking and asks for done rather than scoring you mid-stroke.

The habit being drilled is measured symmetry. Copying the given outline stroke for stroke is the slow way to be wrong; picking three or four heights, reading how far the drawn half sits from the dashed line at each of them, putting a mark that far out on the right and joining the marks is the way symmetry is actually kept — distances from the axis, not an eyeballed shape. After each figure the true mirror is laid over your attempt in lilac, with short whiskers pointing at the two or three widest misses.

The pixel band your strokes have to land in

Your strokes are mirrored back across the line and compared with the true curve by a symmetric point-to-segment chamfer distance, then graded in a band measured in pixels. Anything inside the free zone — 0.4% of the figure's height, or 2.5px, whichever is larger — is still a clean 100. The score falls to zero at 8% of the figure's height or 16px, whichever is larger, with a further pixel allowance added for the hardware in your hand, since a mouse pivots at the wrist and cannot creep. The three figure scores are averaged into the round, and the numbers are only set against your own history. No AI looks at the figure — your strokes are reflected across the line and compared with the true curve point by point, and the average of those distances is the score.
