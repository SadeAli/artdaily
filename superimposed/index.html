A faint guide line with a start dot. Draw that same line 4 times, always from the dot — press anywhere near it and you get snapped onto it. Lift and carry on from where you stopped as often as you like, or press the dot again to start that line over; the line finishes itself at the end ring, or press “finish line”.

Scored against yourself: how tightly your 4 repeats agree (55), how smoothly each one is pulled (30), how closely your starts cluster on the dot (15). Four confident copies of a wrong line beat four timid different ones — that is the whole lesson.

Tip: draw it once with your eyes on the END point, then repeat without correcting. Corrections are what make a line look shaky.

Four copies of the same line

A faint guide line appears with a clear start dot, and you draw that line four times, always from the dot. Each repeat goes down in darker ink, so the fan your own hand makes is there to look at. Four sets, about three minutes, ramping from a short straight to a longer, gently curved line. A repeat can take as many contacts as it needs: press back near where you lifted and the same line carries on, press back on the dot and that repeat starts over. After each set your spread is shaded in around your own mean path, with a verdict in words.

It is the classic Drawabox warm-up, and what it trains is commitment. You are measured against yourself, never against a ruler-straight ideal: four copies that agree beat four careful ones that do not, and that is the whole lesson. The way there is to draw the first one with your eyes on the end point and then repeat without correcting, since mid-stroke corrections are exactly what make a line read as shaky.

The three habits behind the number

Fraying is worth 55: the perpendicular spread of the four repeats around their own mean path, normalised by the guide's length and measured against that path's own tangent, so pulling one repeat faster than another never reads as fraying, and the first half-percent is free. Commitment is worth 30 and reads each stroke's smoothness as turning angle with the mean turn taken out, so an intended curve costs nothing while wobble, corrections and stop-starts show. Start discipline is the last 15, how tightly the four presses cluster on the dot. A round is the mean of its four sets, the zero-points shift with whatever you are drawing on, which the HUD prints, and at the end the weakest of the three is called out.
